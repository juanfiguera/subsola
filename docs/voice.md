# Subsola's voice

**In one sentence:** Subsola sounds like a colleague who has read every record for your street and tells you what's there, plainly and kindly, including what the records can't tell you.

## Traits

| Trait                   | This                                                     | Not that                                                         |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Plain**               | "Each line says where it came from."                     | "Comprehensive provenance metadata is surfaced for every asset." |
| **Specific**            | "Permits name the block, not the spot in the street."    | "Location data may vary in precision."                           |
| **Honest about limits** | "Real lines can sit feet away. Call 811 before you dig." | "Highly accurate utility data you can rely on."                  |
| **Warm, not cute**      | "Spotted a wrong line? Send us a note."                  | "Uh oh, our pipes got crossed!"                                  |

## Rules

**Headings** are short statements in sentence case: "How the map is built", "Before you dig, call 811". No questions except on the FAQ, where the question is the heading. No title case, no puns.

**Buttons and links** start with a verb and say where they go or what they do: "Open the map", "Send feedback", "Copy URL", "Try again". Link the words that name the destination ("the data credits page"), never "click here".

**Errors and empty states** say what happened, what it does and doesn't mean, and what to do next. Never blame the reader. When nothing is drawn, always say that doesn't mean the street is clear.

- "The city's open data didn't respond, so no utilities are shown. That doesn't mean the street is clear."

**Numbers and units:** digits for every count and measurement ("3 ft 5 in", "500 ft", "10 cities"). Abbreviate units after a number (ft, in, m); spell them out in prose without one ("a few feet"). Thousands get commas. Dates in data are ISO (2026-11-14). Never type a figure that the code or data can supply.

**Accuracy and the 811 limit.** Subsola is research, not a utility locate. Say so wherever a reader might act on a line, using those words. Then name the city's one-call center and its notice period. Say "estimated" or "est." whenever a position or depth comes from a standard rather than a record. Say "not published" rather than guessing. Never say or imply "safe to dig", "exact", "precise" or "verified" unless the record itself says so.

**People.** Talk to the reader as "you". Subsola is "Subsola" in most copy and "we" only where a person answers ("We'll change a credit right away"). Use "they" for anyone whose pronouns you don't know.

## Words

| Use                                         | Avoid                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| public records, permits, the city's own map | data assets, intelligence, insights engine |
| estimated, likely, on record, not published | accurate, precise, guaranteed, real-time   |
| research, plan, scope, check                | leverage, unlock, empower, supercharge     |
| line, main, service, pipe, cable            | infrastructure solution, asset layer       |
| free, no account                            | freemium, sign up for free                 |

Also never: em dashes, exclamation marks, "delve", "robust", "seamless", "crucial", "game-changing", "not X, but Y" constructions, or a colon that sets up a reveal.

## Before and after

1. About lede
   - Before: "The pipes and cables under the street, mapped from public records. Made for engineers, contractors, utility coordinators and real estate teams who plan work there."
   - After: "Subsola maps what public records say is under the street, for the engineers, contractors, utility coordinators and real estate teams who plan work there, and for anyone curious about their own block."
2. About, the 811 section
   - Before: "What Subsola shows is what the public record says, not what's in the ground."
   - After: "Subsola shows what the public record says. That isn't always what's in the ground."
3. About, uses
   - Before: "Site research: Which utilities serve a lot, how deep they likely run and who digs there, before the survey."
   - After: "See which utilities serve a lot, how deep they likely run and who digs there, before you order a survey."
4. Map, load failure
   - Before: "Street records could not be loaded. The city's open data did not respond..."
   - After: "Couldn't load street records. The city's open data didn't respond..."
5. Map, check a location
   - Before: "Select the point on the map where you plan to dig to see which utilities may be below and their expected depths."
   - After: "Pick the spot on the map where you plan to dig. You'll see which utilities may be below it and how deep they're expected to be."
6. Explainer heading
   - Before: "Evidence, not a locate"
   - After: "Research, not a utility locate" (the same words everywhere)
7. MCP troubleshooting
   - Before: "Expected: MCP clients use POST, and a plain GET gets 405."
   - After: "That's expected. MCP clients use POST, and a plain GET gets 405."
8. Insights footer link
   - Before: "Ask these records from Claude or another assistant"
   - After: "Look up an address from Claude or another assistant"
